What you can pick in Launceston

The most mapped plants around Launceston, with how many spots are on the map, when they’re usually ready and and how to tell they’re ready.

562

Cherry Plum

Prunus cerasifera

When: Cherry plums usually ripen in early to mid summer, a few weeks before most other plums.

Tip: Pick when the fruit is fully coloured (red, purple or yellow depending on the tree), slightly soft, and comes away easily.

53

Almond

Prunus dulcis

When: Ripe in late summer, when the hulls split open.

Tip: Pick almonds when the green outer hull splits open and begins drying on the tree.

19

Loquat

Eriobotrya japonica

When: Ripe in late winter to spring, one of the first fruits of the year.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully yellow-orange, fragrant, and slightly soft to the touch.

18

Feijoa

Acca sellowiana

When: The main season generally occurs in autumn in temperate regions.

Tip: Pick when fruits drop naturally or come away with a gentle shake of the tree.

17

Lemon

Citrus limon

When: Fruits most of the year, with the main crop in winter to spring.

Tip: A lemon is ripe when it has a bright yellow color, feels firm with a slight give, emits a strong citrus aroma, and is heavy for its size.

15

Apple

Malus domestica

When: Ripe from late summer through autumn, depending on the variety and climate.

Tip: Ripe when fruit is fully coloured, comes off easily with a gentle twist, and seeds are brown.

14

Peach

Prunus persica

When: Ripe from early to late summer, depending on the variety.

Tip: Harvest when fruit is fully coloured, fragrant, and slightly soft around the stem.

11

Plum

Prunus domestica

When: Ripe from mid summer into early autumn, depending on the variety.

Tip: Harvest when fruit is fully coloured, fragrant, and slightly soft near the stem.

8

Fig

Ficus carica

When: Ripe from late summer into autumn.

Tip: Pick when fruit hangs slightly limp, feels soft, and the skin may show slight splitting or drooping.

6

Pear

Pyrus

When: Ripe from late summer through autumn.

Tip: Most European pears (like Packham or Bartlett) are picked when mature but still firm, often while still green, and then ripened off the tree until they soften around the neck first.

4

Rosemary

Salvia rosmarinus

When: Leaves can be picked all year round.

Tip: The edible parts of rosemary include the leaves, flowers, and young tender stems, with leaves being the most commonly used in cooking.

3

Walnut

Juglans regia

When: Nuts fall in autumn when the green husks split.

Tip: Walnuts are typically ripe when the hulls start to split open and the nuts fall to the ground.

Where to look

The most mapped suburbs right now are East Launceston (110), South Launceston (91), Kings Meadows (86), Newnham (80), Trevallyn (67), Norwood (59), Invermay (47) and Mowbray (40). Many more spots are scattered across the rest of Launceston, so check the map for what’s near you.

690 of the edible spots around Launceston are on public land such as street berms, parks and reserves. There are also 17 public drinking water taps and fountains on the map, handy for a long walk or ride. And 3 community gardens, many of which welcome visitors and volunteers.

Forage responsibly

  • Ask before picking from trees on private land, even if branches hang over the footpath.
  • Check your local council’s rules for parks and reserves.
  • Take only what you need and leave plenty for others and for wildlife.
  • Only eat what you can identify with certainty, and wash what you pick, especially near busy roads.
