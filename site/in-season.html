Plants A to Z

The 74 most common edible plants on the Local Pick map, with the number of spots mapped in New Zealand and Australia.

748 spots

Almond

Prunus dulcis

Summer: Ripe in late summer, when the hulls split open.

Tip: Pick almonds when the green outer hull splits open and begins drying on the tree.

2,447 spots

Apple

Malus domestica

Summer and Autumn: Ripe from late summer through autumn, depending on the variety and climate.

Tip: Ripe when fruit is fully coloured, comes off easily with a gentle twist, and seeds are brown.

493 spots

Apricot

Prunus armeniaca

Summer: Ripe in early to mid summer, a short season of a few weeks per tree.

Tip: Ripe apricots develop a rich golden-orange colour and feel slightly soft to the touch.

518 spots

Avocado

Persea americana

Summer and Winter: Fruit matures on the tree for months; depending on the variety it is ready somewhere between late winter and summer. It softens after picking.

Tip: Mature fruit should be full-sized and detach easily from the tree.

217 spots

Banana

Musa acuminata

Summer and Autumn: Bunches can ripen year-round in warm climates, with most in summer and autumn. Cut the bunch when the first fingers start to yellow.

Tip: Pick bananas when the fruit is full-sized and the angles on the fingers have rounded out.

37 spots

Basil

Ocimum basilicum

Spring, Summer and Autumn: A warm-season herb, best from late spring to autumn. Pinch off flowers to keep it leafy.

Tip: While the leaves are typically favored for cooking, other parts of the plant can also be utilized.

500 spots

Bay Leaf

Laurus nobilis

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Leaves can be picked all year round.

Tip: They are used whole, dried, or occasionally ground to flavour dishes.

247 spots

Black Mulberry

Morus nigra

Spring and Summer: Ripe from late spring into summer.

Tip: Pick when the fruit is deep purple-black, very soft, and comes away from the stem with little effort.

539 spots

Black Walnut

Juglans nigra

Autumn: Nuts fall in autumn.

Tip: Ready to pick when the husk has yellowed and softened enough that a firm thumb press leaves a lasting dent, often with dark speckling starting to show and a strong walnut smell.

3,843 spots

Blackberry

Rubus fruticosus

Summer and Autumn: Ripe from mid summer into early autumn.

Tip: Pick when berries are fully black, plump, and come away easily without resistance.

18 spots

Blueberry

Vaccinium corymbosum

Spring and Summer: Ripe from late spring through summer, earlier in warm areas.

Tip: Pick only when berries are fully blue with a matte bloom and detach easily.

19 spots

Cashew

Anacardium occidentale

Spring and Summer: Ripe in late spring to summer in the tropics. The raw shell is toxic, so it must be roasted before eating.

Tip: Cashew fruits are ripe when the fleshy cashew apple turns bright yellow, orange, or red (depending on the variety) and develops a sweet, fruity aroma.

26 spots

Cherimoya

Annona cherimola

Spring, Autumn and Winter: Ripe from late autumn to spring.

Tip: Pick when the fruit is still firm but slightly yielding and green with a faint yellow tinge.

355 spots

Cherry

Prunus avium

Summer: Ripe in early to mid summer; a short season.

Tip: Pick when fully coloured (deep/dark red, or yellow depending on variety) and firm but juicy.

31,113 spots

Cherry Plum

Prunus cerasifera

Summer: Cherry plums usually ripen in early to mid summer, a few weeks before most other plums.

Tip: Pick when the fruit is fully coloured (red, purple or yellow depending on the tree), slightly soft, and comes away easily.

140 spots

Cherry/Red Guava

Psidium cattleianum

Summer and Autumn: Ripe in late summer to autumn.

Tip: Pick when fruit turns deep red (or yellow depending on variety) and is slightly soft to the touch with a strong sweet, floral aroma.

842 spots

Chestnut

Castanea sativa

Autumn: Nuts fall in autumn when the spiky burrs split open.

Tip: Harvest when the burrs split open naturally and nuts fall or can be easily shaken out.

15 spots

Chives

Allium schoenoprasum

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Leaves can be picked from spring to autumn; the plant dies back in winter. Flowers appear in late spring and summer.

Tip: The leaves, flowers, and stems of chives are all edible, with the leaves being the most commonly used for their mild onion flavour.

21 spots

Cilantro

Coriandrum sativum

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Grows best in the cooler months (autumn to spring); it runs to seed quickly in summer heat.

Tip: The bright green herb grows on a long stem with a delicate, flat flower-like leaf, almost resembling a frilly, large clover.

15 spots

Crabapple

Malus

Summer and Autumn: Ripe in late summer to autumn.

Tip: Crabapples are ready to pick when they have developed their full colour, come away from the tree with a gentle twist, and the seeds inside are dark brown rather than pale.

218 spots

Curly Parsley

Petroselinum crispum

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Leaves can be picked most of the year; it goes to seed in its second spring.

Tip: To prepare, rinse the leaves thoroughly, pat dry, and finely chop them.

52 spots

Curry Leaf Tree

Bergera koenigii

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Leaves can be picked all year in warm climates; growth slows and some leaves drop in cold winters.

Tip: The fresh leaves are an indispensable part of Indian cuisine and Indian traditional medicines.

36 spots

Dill

Anethum graveolens

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Best from spring to autumn. Leaves are picked young; seeds ripen in summer.

Tip: You can eat virtually the entire dill plant, however the leaves are most commonly used for their bright herbaceous flavour.

26 spots

Dragonfruit

Selenicereus undatus

Summer and Autumn: Fruits in flushes from summer to autumn.

Tip: Pick when skin colour is vibrant pink or yellow and “flaps” (bracts) are slightly withered but still fresh-looking.

942 spots

Elderberry

Sambucus nigra

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Flowers in late spring; berries ripen in late summer to early autumn. Cook the berries before eating.

Tip: Harvest when clusters are fully dark purple-black and all berries in the bunch are ripe.

1,277 spots

Feijoa

Acca sellowiana

Autumn: The main season generally occurs in autumn in temperate regions. In warmer climates, the season can extend from late summer into winter.

Tip: Pick when fruits drop naturally or come away with a gentle shake of the tree.

2,699 spots

Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Leaves and fronds can be picked from spring to autumn; flowers in summer and seeds ripen in late summer to autumn.

Tip: Big white bulb with green stalks and feathery leaves.

1,334 spots

Fig

Ficus carica

Summer and Autumn: Ripe from late summer into autumn. Some trees also give a small early crop in early summer.

Tip: Pick when fruit hangs slightly limp, feels soft, and the skin may show slight splitting or drooping.

82 spots

Finger Lime

Citrus australasica

Summer, Autumn and Winter: Ripe from late summer to winter, varying between trees.

Tip: Finger limes are ripe when they feel slightly soft to the touch, have a subtle sheen on the skin, emit a pleasant citrus aroma, and detach easily from the tree.

35 spots

Gold Kiwifruit

Actinidia chinensis

Autumn and Winter: Ripe in autumn to early winter, picked a little earlier than green kiwifruit.

Tip: Harvest when the fruit is full-sized and slightly soft at the stem end, but still firm overall.

104 spots

Gooseberry

Ribes

Summer: Ripe in early to mid summer.

Tip: Gooseberries can be picked slightly underripe when firm and green for cooking, or left on the bush until they develop their full colour (green, yellow, red, or purple depending on the variety) and become slightly soft for eating fresh.

190 spots

Grape

Vitis vinifera

Summer and Autumn: Ripe from late summer to autumn, depending on the variety.

Tip: Harvest when berries are fully coloured, sweet, and easily detach from the stem.

141 spots

Grapefruit

Citrus paradisi

Spring and Winter: Ripe from winter through spring and holds well on the tree.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully coloured, heavy, and slightly springy when pressed.

187 spots

Guava

Psidium guajava

Summer, Autumn and Winter: Ripe from late summer into winter, with some fruit at other times in the tropics.

Tip: Pick when fruit gives slightly to pressure and emits a strong sweet fragrance.

91 spots

Hazelnut

Corylus americana

Summer and Autumn: Ripe in late summer to early autumn, when the husks brown and nuts drop.

Tip: Hazelnuts are ready to harvest in late summer to early autumn when the husks turn from green to yellow-brown and the nuts begin dropping naturally from the tree.

26 spots

Jackfruit

Artocarpus heterophyllus

Summer and Autumn: Ripe in summer to autumn, sometimes a second smaller crop.

Tip: Harvest when the fruit develops a strong aroma, dull green-to-yellowing skin, and slight softening with spines flattening.

42 spots

Japanese Walnut

Juglans ailantifolia

Autumn: Nuts fall in autumn.

Tip: Ready to pick when the fuzzy, sticky husk shifts from green to a yellowish-brown and softens, usually still hanging in its distinctive cluster of several nuts.

31 spots

Kiwifruit

Actinidia chinensis var. deliciosa

Autumn: Ripe in late autumn. Fruit is picked firm and softens off the vine.

Tip: Kiwifruit are typically picked in late autumn when they are full-sized but still firm, and the seeds are black and fully developed inside.

23 spots

Kumquat

Citrus japonica

Spring and Winter: Ripe in winter to spring.

Tip: Harvest when the skin is fully orange and slightly glossy, and the fruit feels just a little soft.

1,506 spots

Lemon

Citrus limon

Spring and Winter: Fruits most of the year, with the main crop in winter to spring.

Tip: A lemon is ripe when it has a bright yellow color, feels firm with a slight give, emits a strong citrus aroma, and is heavy for its size.

158 spots

Lime

Citrus × latifolia, C. aurantiifolia

Summer, Autumn and Winter: Ripe from late summer into winter, with some fruit at other times.

Tip: A lime is ripe when it feels heavy, slightly soft to the touch, has smooth, glossy skin, and emits a fresh, zesty aroma, with colour ranging from vibrant green to slightly yellow depending on the variety.

1,866 spots

Loquat

Eriobotrya japonica

Spring and Winter: Ripe in late winter to spring, one of the first fruits of the year.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully yellow-orange, fragrant, and slightly soft to the touch.

359 spots

Macadamia

Macadamia integrifolia

Summer, Autumn and Winter: Nuts fall mainly from late summer to winter.

Tip: Harvest when nuts drop naturally or husks split open on the tree.

238 spots

Mandarin

Citrus reticulata

Autumn and Winter: Ripe in late autumn through winter, depending on the variety.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully orange, fragrant, and slightly soft with a heavy feel.

535 spots

Mango

Mangifera indica

Spring and Summer: Ripe from late spring to late summer, starting earlier in the tropics than in subtropical areas.

Tip: Harvest when the fruit reaches full size and starts to show colour change or a slight yellow blush, depending on variety.

154 spots

Mint

Mentha spicata

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Leaves can be picked from spring to autumn; it dies back in cold winters.

Tip: The leaf, fresh or dried, is the culinary source of mint.

132 spots

Mulberry

Morus alba

Spring and Summer: Ripe from late spring into summer.

Tip: Harvest when berries are fully dark purple-black (or deep red depending on variety) and fall easily with a gentle touch.

44 spots

Nashi/Asian Pear

Pyrus pyrifolia

Summer and Autumn: Ripe in late summer to early autumn. Ripens on the tree and is eaten crisp.

Tip: Nashi pears are ready to pick when they reach full size and their skin colour shifts slightly toward a golden-yellow (depending on variety), often with a sweet fragrance developing.

21 spots

Nectarine

Prunus persica var. nucipersica

Summer: Ripe from early to late summer, depending on the variety.

Tip: Harvest when fruit is fully coloured, fragrant, and slightly soft near the stem.

42 spots

Olive

Olea europaea

Summer, Autumn and Winter: Green olives can be picked in late summer; fully ripe black olives in late autumn to winter. Cure before eating.

Tip: Pick depending on use: green for firmer, bitter olives or black/purple for fully ripened fruit with richer oil content.

318 spots

Orange

Citrus sinensis

Spring, Summer and Winter: Navel oranges ripen in winter; Valencia oranges in spring to summer. Fruit holds well on the tree.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully coloured, heavy, and slightly springy when squeezed.

32 spots

Oregano

Origanum vulgare

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Leaves can be picked from spring to autumn, with the best flavour just before flowering in summer.

Tip: For the best flavour, pick the top 5–10 cm of the plant before it flowers.

61 spots

Papaya

Carica papaya

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Fruits year-round in warm climates, most heavily in summer and autumn.

Tip: Harvest when skin begins turning yellow-orange and fruit softens slightly at the blossom end.

1,140 spots

Passionfruit

Passiflora edulis

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Main crop in summer to autumn, often a smaller crop in spring. Ripe when it falls or the skin wrinkles.

Tip: Pick when fruit changes from green to deep purple or yellow and often falls from the vine naturally.

1,073 spots

Peach

Prunus persica

Summer: Ripe from early to late summer, depending on the variety.

Tip: Harvest when fruit is fully coloured, fragrant, and slightly soft around the stem.

559 spots

Pear

Pyrus

Summer and Autumn: Ripe from late summer through autumn. European pears are picked firm and ripened off the tree.

Tip: Most European pears (like Packham or Bartlett) are picked when mature but still firm, often while still green, and then ripened off the tree until they soften around the neck first.

60 spots

Pecan

Carya illinoinensis

Autumn: Nuts fall in late autumn when the husks split.

Tip: Harvest when the husks split open and nuts drop naturally or shake loose easily.

90 spots

Peppermint

Mentha x piperita

Spring, Summer and Autumn: Leaves can be picked from spring to autumn; it dies back in cold winters.

Tip: The primary parts of the peppermint plant used are the leaves, flowers, and essential oil, which are utilized for culinary, medicinal, and aromatic purposes.

110 spots

Persimmon

Diospyros kaki

Autumn: Ripe in autumn, usually after the leaves start colouring and dropping.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully coloured and starting to soften, but still holds its shape—unless it’s a astringent type, which should be left until very soft.

279 spots

Pinenut

Pinus

Autumn: Cones ripen in autumn and open as they dry.

Tip: Pick mature cones before they fully open and lose their seeds, then dry them in a warm, airy place until the scales open and the nuts can be removed.

1,226 spots

Plum

Prunus domestica

Summer and Autumn: Ripe from mid summer into early autumn, depending on the variety.

Tip: Harvest when fruit is fully coloured, fragrant, and slightly soft near the stem.

214 spots

Pomegranate

Punica granatum

Autumn: Ripe in autumn.

Tip: Pomegranates are typically ready to harvest in late autumn when the skin deepens to a rich red (or yellow-red depending on variety), becomes slightly matte rather than glossy, and the fruit feels heavy for its size.

154 spots

Quince

Cydonia oblonga

Autumn: Ripe in autumn, when the fruit turns fully yellow and fragrant. Usually cooked.

Tip: Harvest when fruit turns fully yellow and develops a strong floral aroma.

577 spots

Rosemary

Salvia rosmarinus

Spring and Winter: Leaves can be picked all year round. Flowers mostly in winter and spring.

Tip: The edible parts of rosemary include the leaves, flowers, and young tender stems, with leaves being the most commonly used in cooking.

31 spots

Sage

Salvia officinalis

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Leaves can be picked all year, with the most growth in spring and summer.

Tip: The herb is soft in texture, almost like felt or velvet.

125 spots

Serviceberry

Amelanchier canadensis

Summer: Ripe in early summer.

Tip: Pick when berries are fully purple-blue to dark red (depending on species) and soft with a sweet flavour.

36 spots

Sour Cherry

Prunus cerasus

Summer: Ripe in early to mid summer.

Tip: Harvest when fruit is fully red and juicy, slightly soft but still firm enough to hold shape.

40 spots

Strawberry

Fragaria x ananassa

Spring and Summer: Ripe from late spring through summer.

Tip: Harvest when fully red, glossy, and evenly coloured with no white or green shoulders.

56 spots

Tamarillo

Solanum betaceum

Autumn and Winter: Ripe in late autumn to winter.

Tip: Pick when fruit is fully coloured (red, orange, or purple depending on variety) and slightly soft.

74 spots

Tamarind

Tamarindus indica

Spring and Winter: Pods ripen in late winter to spring and can stay on the tree for weeks.

Tip: Harvest when pods are brown, brittle, and dry on the outside, with pulp pulling away from the shell.

75 spots

Thyme

Thymus vulgaris

Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter: Leaves can be picked all year round, with the most growth in spring and summer.

Tip: The leaves are the most commonly used part in cooking, offering an earthy, slightly floral flavour with hints of lemon and mint.

737 spots

Walnut

Juglans regia

Autumn: Nuts fall in autumn when the green husks split.

Tip: Walnuts are typically ripe when the hulls start to split open and the nuts fall to the ground.

1,443 spots

White Mulberry

Morus alba

Spring and Summer: Ripe from late spring into summer.

Tip: Pick when the fruit turns creamy white to pale pink (depending on the variety), becomes soft, and tastes sweet rather than tart.

35 spots

White Sapote

Casimiroa edulis

Spring, Autumn and Winter: Ripe in late autumn to spring.

Tip: Pick white sapote when the fruit is full-sized and begins to soften slightly or can be detached with a gentle lift.

Questions

What fruit is in season in New Zealand and Australia right now?

Check the season highlighted at the top of this page. In spring look for loquats, mulberries and the first citrus; in summer, cherry plums, stone fruit, blackberries and passionfruit; in autumn, feijoas, apples, figs, walnuts and chestnuts; and in winter, citrus such as lemons, mandarins and oranges.

When are feijoas in season?

Feijoas are an autumn fruit in most of New Zealand and southern Australia, roughly March to May. In warmer places the season can run from late summer into winter.

Do seasons differ between cities?

Yes. Fruit ripens earlier in warmer places like Brisbane, Perth and Auckland than in Melbourne, Christchurch or Dunedin, and the tropical north follows wet and dry seasons instead. Ripeness updates from local foragers in the Local Pick app show what’s ready near you.
